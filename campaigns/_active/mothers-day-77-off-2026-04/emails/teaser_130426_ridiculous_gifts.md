"Six-pack of craft beer. Box of dark chocolate."

"That's not bad," Alice said.

"It's her favorite. I'm not overthinking it."

(Must be nice.)

Then Jade went. Our designer. The woman who picks buckles like she's choosing weapons. She took a breath and said:

"I once gave my mom a rock."

Silence.

"From a beach in Maine. I told her it was from a high-end home décor brand. Scandinavian. Very Scandinavian energy, you know."

"JADE. YOU LIAR."

"It's been on her windowsill for four years."

(The rock was inspired. I said what I said.)

Then it was Alice's turn.

Alice took a long, theatrical sip of coffee. Looked at us dead in the eye. And said:

"I gave her a strip club ticket."

"ALICE. NO. WHERE. WHEN. ALICE—"

She just giggled. Wouldn't confirm. Wouldn't deny. The only thing I know is her mom texted her "best year yet, baby" the next morning and I'm afraid to ask follow-up questions.

(I'm choosing to believe the ticket was real. It's funnier that way.)

I was laughing so hard my mascara migrated south and sat down on my cheekbones for a rest. And then I stopped laughing.

Because I realized I had one too.

I was eight. I made my mom a "protection potion." Mason jar, tap water, red food coloring, a handful of glitter from the craft drawer, and one dead ladybug I found on the windowsill. I labeled it "MOM'S PROTECTION POTION — DO NOT DRINK" in purple Sharpie. My handwriting was unhinged. I was very proud.

She put it on the kitchen shelf like it was a Le Creuset.

Last Christmas I was home, digging through a junk drawer for tape, and I found it. Still sealed. Still labeled. The water had evaporated down to a rusty pink crust and a shriveled ladybug in a pile of glitter. My 8-year-old handwriting was the exact same handwriting I'd used to sign my first Skullette business cards.

She kept it. Twenty-something years. A jar of food dye and a dead bug.

I had to leave the room for a minute.

Jade's rock. Marcus's beer. Alice's ticket (allegedly). My dead-bug potion.
